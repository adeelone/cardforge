A business card has about five seconds to do its job: remind someone who you are, and make it effortless to contact you. Everything on the card should serve one of those two goals. Here's what earns its space in 2026, and what doesn't.

## The essentials

1. **Your name.** The largest text on the card. Use the name people will actually search for.
2. **What you do.** A job title, or a plain description like "Wedding photographer". If your official title means nothing outside your company, describe the work instead.
3. **Company or brand.** Include it if it helps people place you; a logo can replace it.
4. **One primary way to reach you.** Usually email. Pick the channel you actually reply to.
5. **A website or portfolio link.** Keep it short, and drop the `https://www.`.
6. **A QR code.** It turns the card into a saved contact or a digital card in one scan. See [how to make a digital business card with a QR code](/blog/digital-business-card-qr-code).

## Useful, depending on your work

- **Mobile number:** expected in sales, real estate, and trades. Optional elsewhere.
- **One or two social profiles:** only the ones that matter for work. LinkedIn for most professionals; Instagram or Behance for visual work.
- **Pronouns:** a growing norm, and helpful for anyone writing to you.
- **A tagline:** one short line saying what you're known for, e.g. "Brand systems for small teams."
- **Physical address:** for shops, studios, and offices that people visit.

## What to leave off

- **Fax numbers**, unless your industry still runs on them.
- **More than two phone numbers.** Pick one.
- **Every social network you've ever joined.** Two at most.
- **Your home address**, especially on cards you hand to strangers.
- **Taglines that could belong to anyone**, like "Passionate about excellence".
- **Tiny legal text** on the front. If something must be included, put it on the back.

## Layout rules that make cards readable

- **Clear hierarchy:** the name is largest, the role second, and contact details are small but legible (at least **7–8 pt**).
- **Plenty of space:** a crowded card reads as a flyer. If you're out of room, use the back.
- **Front for identity, back for action:** name and brand on the front; contact details and QR code on the back.
- **Contrast:** keep text at a WCAG AA contrast ratio against its background. CardForge checks this for you as you design.
- **Respect the safe zone:** keep text at least 3–5 mm inside the trim line. The [business card size and bleed guide](/blog/business-card-size-and-bleed) explains why.

## Before you print: a 60-second check

- [ ] Every phone number and email is spelled correctly. Have someone else read it aloud.
- [ ] The website loads and is the one you want to show.
- [ ] The QR code scans from a printed proof with two different phones.
- [ ] Text is at least 7 pt and passes a contrast check.
- [ ] The file is a PDF with bleed and crop marks.
